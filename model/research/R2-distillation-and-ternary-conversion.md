# ANGLE 2 — Distillation and compression into small/ternary students (2024–2026)

*Aefinity AI research brief, 2026-10-06. All numbers are from the cited primary sources; cost figures marked "est." are my 6·N·D FLOP estimates, not measurements.*

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Ternary-from-dense is now a solved engineering problem at the task level, not at the general level.** BitDistill (Microsoft, 2025-10-15) converts Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B to ternary with only **10B tokens** of continued pre-training plus logit + attention distillation and matches FP16 on its downstream tasks; generic ternarisation of Qwen3-1.7B (PrismML Bonsai) retains 88% of average score but only **71% of BFCLv3 tool-calling** (51.0 vs 71.8). Tool-calling is the capability that ternarisation hurts most at 1–2B, so ALICE-Next must distil *tool behaviour* explicitly after ternarisation.
2. **On-policy (reverse-KL) distillation is the cheapest known post-training signal**: Qwen3 reports 74.4 AIME'24 for 1,800 GPU-h vs 67.6 for 17,920 GPU-h of RL (≈10×); DeepSeek-R1 shows SFT-distillation beating large-scale RL on a 32B student (72.6 vs 47.0 AIME). It requires a **shared tokenizer** between teacher and student — this single constraint decides the base model.
3. **Provenance**: Qwen3 (Apache-2.0), Qwen3.5 (Apache-2.0), Gemma 4 (Apache-2.0), Ministral 3 (Apache-2.0), DeepSeek-R1 (MIT), Phi-4-mini (MIT), BitNet-b1.58-2B-4T (MIT), OLMo 2/3 (Apache-2.0, open data) are clean teachers/bases. **Gemma ≤3n** (distillation output explicitly makes a "Model Derivative"), **Llama 3.x/4** ("Llama" name prefix if you train on outputs; 700M MAU), **OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini API** (ToS forbid training competing models from Output) are not.
4. **Cheapest credible path** to a 1–3B ternary tool expert: a Qwen3-1.7B (or 4B) student, BitDistill-style ternarisation (SubLN + 10–30B-token CPT on an open corpus with logit KD from FP16 Qwen3), then tool SFT on Apache/CC-BY tool-trajectory data and on-policy distillation from Qwen3-8B/32B. Est. 150–500 rented H100-hours (≈ $0.4k–1.5k). Everything in the chain is Apache-2.0; the only obligation is attribution.

**Logit vs sequence-level KD, in one line:** logit/on-policy KD gives dense per-token signal and the 10× compute wins above, but needs a shared tokenizer; sequence-level (SFT on teacher outputs, R1/OpenThoughts style) is tokenizer-agnostic and is what every public ≤3B reasoning model actually used. MiniPLM is the only published cross-tokenizer method with pre-training-scale evidence.

## 5. Candidate paths, costed

Assumptions: FLOPs ≈ 6·N·D for training, 2·N·D for teacher forwards; rented H100 at ~350–400 TFLOP/s sustained, $2–3/h. Kaggle T4 (~20 TFLOP/s sustained, 30 h/week) covers only the SFT/OPD stages below, never the CPT stage.

**Path A — Fine-tune the existing BitNet-b1.58-2B-4T (weeks, <$300).** Start from the MIT bf16 master weights; SFT on ~200k Toucan/Nemotron tool trajectories (~0.5B tokens → est. ~6 H100-h), then reinforcement with verifiable tool rewards (schema-valid call, correct tool, correct args) and abstention rewards. *Limitation:* its Llama-3 tokenizer means logit-level/on-policy KD teachers would have to be Llama models (licence taint); only sequence-level KD from Qwen3 is clean. 4,096 context. Provenance: MIT base (Microsoft's 4T-token data mix undisclosed) + Apache data. Already CIS-1-native (ReLU², SubLN, RoPE). This is the right **tool-call baseline** and a shippable interim.

**Path B — BitDistill-style ternary Qwen3-1.7B (or 4B) + tool distillation (1–2 months, est. $0.4k–1.5k).** (1) Insert SubLN before the attention/FFN output projections as in BitDistill; (2) 10B tokens CPT on an open corpus (Dolma 3 / FineWeb-Edu / Nemotron-CC; MidTool-style tool mid-training mix) with logit + attention KD from FP16 Qwen3-1.7B or Qwen3-4B (BitDistill shows a larger teacher beats same-size FP16) — est. 6·1.7e9·1e10 ≈ 1.0e20 FLOP ≈ 75–100 H100-h incl. ~20% BitNet overhead; stretch to 30B tokens (ParetoQ saturation) ≈ 250–300 H100-h; (3) tool SFT on Toucan + Nemotron-Agentic + OpenThoughts-Agent (est. 10–20 H100-h); (4) on-policy reverse-KL distillation from Qwen3-8B (same tokenizer) on tool prompts plus abstention/"I don't know" and LOOKUP-use prompts (est. 50–100 H100-h incl. decode inefficiency); (5) prune the 151,936 vocab to the ASCII subset as ALICE already does, re-freeze, re-measure. Qwen3-1.7B is 28 layers, GQA 16/8, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE — standard ops, CIS-1-expressible; 1.4B non-embedding params ternary ≈ 0.3 GB + pruned embeddings, well inside 1.4 GB (4B would also fit: Bonsai-4B is 1.02 GB at 2.125 b/w). Expected ceiling from Bonsai: ≥88% of Qwen3-1.7B average before tool re-distillation; BitDistill suggests near-parity on distilled behaviours. Provenance: Apache-2.0 throughout; attribution only.

**Path C — From-scratch ternary (sovereign line; months, $5k–25k).** Spectra-1.1's exponents say a 1.5B TriLM wants ≥1T tokens: 6·1.5e9·1.2e12 ≈ 1.1e22 FLOP ≈ 8,000 H100-h (≈$20k+); a Spectra-1-scale 300B-token run ≈ 2,000 H100-h (≈$5k) lands near FloatLM parity only at ~3.9B. Data: Dolma 3 (Apache-2.0) keeps the chain fully open. Tool expertise still needs stage (3)–(4) from Path B, and ALICE-30M's +0.38 nat gap is consistent with the literature's ≥1B / 100B+ parity condition. Not the cheapest; it is the proof.

**Path D — Fine-tune Falcon-E-1B/3B (fast, ~$100).** Already ternary, 1.5T tokens, onebitllms example: 8×A10G, 8.5 h for a full SFT. Costs: Falcon-LLM licence attribution + AUP pass-through; no SubLN-style layout (engine changes); vocab 32,678; full fine-tuning only. A credible fallback if Path B's CPT budget is unavailable.

**Recommendation.** Run A now on the receipt harness (it is also the BitNet-2B tool-call baseline you already queued). Build ALICE-Next on **Path B with Qwen3-1.7B** as the air-gapped SKU and Qwen3-4B as the connected SKU if the 1.4 GB budget allows; use the Spectra-1.1 data-over-parameters finding to justify spending tokens on CPT rather than a larger student. Keep Path C as the funded sovereign milestone. File PUBLISH? before any Apache-attributed distilled weights leave the archive, per policy.

## 6. Engine notes (CIS-1)

- BitDistill's SubLN and BitNet's per-tensor absmean scale map onto ALICE's existing fixed-point norm path; Bonsai's g128 FP16 scales would need a per-group integer scale table (cheap, but new) and cost +0.125 b/w.
- Logit KD before vocab pruning: compute KL over the full 151,936 teacher vocab, then prune; or mask teacher logits to the retained ASCII subset and renormalise — the latter is what the connected variant will see at inference anyway.
- Qwen3.5/Gemma 4 hybrids are teachers, not bases, until Gated-DeltaNet / 512-window local attention exist as exact-integer kernels.

## Key claims (as returned by the research agent, with sources)
- BitNet Distillation (BitDistill, submitted 2025-10-15) converts Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B to 1.58-bit using SubLN, 10B tokens of continued pre-training from the FALCON corpus, and MiniLM-style attention + logit distillation; it reports 88–96% accuracy on MNLI/QNLI/SST-2 matching FP16-SFT (vs 74–82% for direct 1.58-bit SFT), 10x memory saving (0.11 vs 1.20 GB) and 2.65x CPU speed (1,135 vs 427 tok/s).  
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998>
- ParetoQ finds optimal performance is nearly achieved by spending the majority of the budget on FP training and ~10% on QAT; 1-bit/1.58-bit/2-bit saturate at ~30B QAT tokens while 3/4-bit saturate at ~10B; its 600M ternary model (58.7 avg) surpasses a prior 3B ternary model trained with 100B tokens; released weights carry the FAIR Noncommercial Research License.  
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02631>
- Spectra-1.1 fits a ternary scaling law with training-data exponent 0.81 versus parameter exponent 0.32 (FloatLM 0.53 vs 0.56), and trains 1.5B/2.5B/3.6B TriLMs on up to 1.2T tokens (paper submitted 2025-06-28).  
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23025>
- Qwen3 Technical Report: on Qwen3-8B, on-policy distillation reached AIME'24 74.4 / AIME'25 65.5 in ~1,800 GPU-hours versus 67.6 / 55.5 for RL in 17,920 GPU-hours; all Qwen3 models are released under Apache 2.0.  
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388>
- DeepSeek-R1 distilled students were fine-tuned with ~800K curated samples using SFT only (no RL); DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scored 72.6 AIME 2024 / 94.3 MATH-500 / 62.1 GPQA / 57.2 LiveCodeBench versus 47.0 / 91.6 / 55.0 / 40.2 for DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B trained with large-scale RL.  
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1>
- PrismML Ternary Bonsai 1.7B (Apache-2.0, built from Qwen3-1.7B, GGUF Q2_0 at 2.125 bits/weight, 436 MiB) scores 58.47 average vs 66.57 for Qwen3-1.7B, with BFCLv3 51.0 vs 71.8 and GSM8K 74.2 vs 83.1; the whitepaper discloses format and evaluation but not the training recipe or token count.  
  <https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-1.7B-gguf>
- The Gemma Terms of Use define Model Derivatives to include models created through distillation or 'methods based on the generation of synthetic data Outputs by Gemma', require pass-through of use restrictions, cover Gemma 1, 1.1, 2, 3 and 3n, and state that Gemma 4 has a separate license (Apache 2.0 per the Gemma 4 E2B model card).  
  <https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms>
- The Llama 3.2 Community License (release date 2024-09-25) states that if you use the Llama Materials 'or any outputs or results' to create, train, fine tune or improve an AI model that is distributed, you must include 'Llama' at the beginning of the model name, display 'Built with Llama', and request a license from Meta above 700 million monthly active users.  
  <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meta-llama/llama-models/main/models/llama3_2/LICENSE>
- Hugging Face's Llama3-8B-1.58-100B-tokens was fine-tuned from Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct on 100B FineWeb-edu tokens (10B warm-up + 45,000 steps at 2M tokens/step, peak LR 1e-5) and is described as close to Llama3 8B on some metrics but behind in overall average.  
  <https://huggingface.co/HF1BitLLM/Llama3-8B-1.58-100B-tokens>
- 'Capability-Stratified Degradation in Ternary Language Models' (2026-08-28) ternarised Qwen3.5-0.8B (752M params) with 72.4M QAT tokens, retaining 77.1% of teacher performance on ten tasks while MMLU linear-probe recall fell to 26.19% vs 43.76% for the teacher, concluding ternary conversion is unsuitable as a drop-in general replacement but valuable as a substrate for task-specific models.  
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28809>
